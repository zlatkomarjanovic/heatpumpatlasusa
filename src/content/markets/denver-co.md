If you are replacing a Denver furnace and an aging air conditioner, price a cold-climate heat pump against the cost of replacing both. If you are replacing the furnace alone, compare the running costs carefully before you decide.

## What you might pay to install

| Project | Modeled installed price before incentives | Scope |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Install a cold-climate ducted heat pump | $14,400 to $33,600 | Cold-climate equipment using existing ducts |
| Reuse ducts for a like-for-like replacement | $8,400 to $19,200 | Existing ducts and a straightforward equipment swap |
| Install a multi-zone mini split | $7,800 to $24,000 | Several indoor heads and no new duct network |

The ranges apply the model’s West factor of 1.20 to national equipment bands. Ask for a line item for backup heat and panel work. Ask the installer how much heat the exact model delivers on a cold Denver day and what would cover the difference if it falls short. These are planning ranges, not a survey of local bids. They exclude major duct repairs or new ductwork and panel or service upgrades. See the [national installation cost guide](/heat-pump-cost/) for scope assumptions.

## What the heat could cost each year

Our Colorado references are 17.13¢/kWh for electricity (June 2026) and $11.92/Mcf for gas (May 2026). On those assumptions, a gas furnace can be cheaper to run than a ducted heat pump. Compare your own bill and the cost of replacing your air conditioner before spending money based on a projected annual saving.

## Check rebates before you sign

Use the [Xcel Colorado incentive guide](/rebates/xcel-energy/) and verify the current offer and equipment requirements with your utility. Check [Colorado Energy Office heat pump tax credits](https://energyoffice.colorado.gov/hptc) separately. Ask for incentives to be itemized and confirm what can be combined before you subtract anything.
