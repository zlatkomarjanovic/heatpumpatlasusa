The Massachusetts heat pump rebate people search is Mass Save. Massachusetts is the most mature heat pump incentive market in the country, and Mass Save is why. The program has been running long enough that most contractors in the state know how to work with it, which sounds like a small thing until you talk to a homeowner in a state where nobody has done the paperwork before.

The size of the rebate is the headline, but the **order of operations** is what actually determines whether you get paid.

## What the program pays

Mass Save splits air source heat pump projects into two categories, and the difference in rebate is large enough that it should influence your system design conversation.

A **whole-home** installation treats the heat pump as the primary heating system, with capacity sized to meet the home's design heating load. That is the tier that reaches the upper end of the range.

A **partial-home** installation covers part of the house, typically leaving an existing system in place for other zones or for backup. It receives a lower rebate, but it is also a much cheaper project, so the percentage of cost covered is not as different as the raw numbers suggest. There is also a $500 sizing bonus for partial-home projects that are nonetheless sized to meet the full heating load.

There is a detail here that matters and that contractors do not always raise. A system sized to meet the full load costs more in equipment but can qualify for a larger rebate and avoids the backup heat draw that drives up operating cost in January. Ask for the sizing documentation.

## The reservation requirement

This is the part that catches people. Mass Save generally requires a rebate commitment letter to be reserved before installation, and the letter is valid for a limited window. If the work happens first, the project can be ineligible.

The practical instruction is simple: before you sign a contract, confirm in writing that the rebate is reserved. If a contractor is vague about this, that is a signal about how the rest of the project will go.

## Stacking and the operating cost rate

Massachusetts households on a qualifying heat pump can access a seasonal electric rate that is lower than the standard residential rate, and over ten years that rate can be worth more than the one-time rebate. This is the single most under-discussed part of the Massachusetts heat pump case.

Federal HEAR funding is also allocated to Massachusetts, and income-qualified households may be able to combine programs. The stacking rules have moved during 2026, so treat any combined figure you see online, including ours, as requiring confirmation rather than as a promise.

## Is this worth pursuing?

Massachusetts sits in a difficult spot on operating economics: electricity is expensive at 29.61 cents per kilowatt hour, and natural gas is also expensive at $31.39 per Mcf. That combination means the heat pump case in Massachusetts depends heavily on what you are replacing.

Against **oil**, which heats roughly a quarter of Massachusetts homes, the case is strong. Against gas, it is closer, and the seasonal heat pump rate is often what tips it. Run the [calculator](/calculator/) with your actual usage before deciding, and check the [oil comparison](/heat-pump-vs-oil/) for the fuel-specific math.

## Mass Save heat pump installation in Massachusetts

The search that keeps showing up is Mass Save heat pump installation in Massachusetts. The install itself is a normal air source job. The paperwork is the part that is different.

A participating contractor files the reservation, documents whole-home or partial-home sizing, and holds the commitment letter while the equipment goes in. If that letter is missing, the Mass Save heat pump rebate can disappear even when the machine is already on the wall. Ask for the reservation number before a deposit.

Typical Massachusetts installed cost still sits on the [heat pump cost](/heat-pump-cost/) bands. The rebate comes off that invoice, not instead of it.

## Massachusetts heat pump tax credit versus Mass Save

People still search for a Massachusetts heat pump tax credit from 2025. That query is almost always the old federal 25C credit, not a separate state income-tax line. 25C is closed for 2026 installs. The live Massachusetts offer is the Mass Save rebate, with HEAR only for households that still qualify.

If a quote still subtracts $2,000 of federal tax credit, send the contractor the [heat pump tax credit](/heat-pump-tax-credit/) page and re-run the number.
