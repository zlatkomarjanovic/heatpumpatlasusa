Colorado is the best stacking opportunity outside the Northeast, and the reason is unusual: three separate pots of money with different eligibility rules can land on the same project.

## The three programs

**Xcel Energy** pays a residential heat pump rebate that generally ranges from about $400 to $2,400 depending on equipment tier and whether the home is currently gas-heated. On its own it is modest.

**Colorado HEAR** is the larger number. The state program pays $3,000 for a standard heat pump and up to $8,000 for qualifying cold-climate equipment, with income limits applying to the higher tier. That cold-climate differential is worth pausing on, because it is a genuinely good piece of policy design. A standard heat pump in a 5,500 degree day climate will lean heavily on backup heat in January and deliver a worse result than the model promised. The program pays more for the equipment that actually solves that.

**The state tax credit** is claimed by a registered contractor at roughly $1,000 per four tons of installed capacity and passed through on your invoice. Your contractor has to be registered, and if they are not, you lose the credit entirely.

There is also the **Power Ahead Colorado** rebate, worth $1,500 in the Denver region for qualifying ENERGY STAR equipment.

## The honest operating cost picture

Colorado gas is cheap. At around $11.92 per Mcf, it is among the least expensive residential gas in the country. That makes the gas-to-heat-pump operating cost case harder than you might expect from a cold-climate state.

Electricity is moderate at 17.13 cents per kilowatt hour, so the heat pump is not at a severe disadvantage, but a 90 percent gas furnace in Colorado is genuinely competitive on running cost. The [furnace comparison](/heat-pump-vs-furnace/) shows Colorado in the contested range.

What carries the Colorado case is the incentive stack and the cooling benefit. A heat pump replaces the air conditioner in a state where summer cooling matters, which changes the comparison entirely when the AC is at end of life.

## Sequencing a Colorado project

Confirm your contractor is registered for the state tax credit, because that is the easiest benefit to lose. Confirm the HEAR tier you qualify for, since the income gate is the difference between $3,000 and $8,000. Then verify the Xcel rebate against your equipment selection, since the tiers are tied to specific efficiency ratings.
