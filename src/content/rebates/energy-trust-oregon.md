The **Energy Trust of Oregon heat pump rebate** is a smaller check than the Northeast programs, and that is fine. Oregon's case is usually the operating cost, not the incentive.

## What Energy Trust pays

We model **$400 to $2,500**. Existing fuel, equipment tier and ductwork move the number. A ducted replacement that also touches ducts pays more than a simple equipment swap. A household leaving gas can see a different code than a household leaving electric resistance.

You generally need to be a customer of a participating Oregon utility and use an Energy Trust trade ally contractor. If either of those is missing, assume the rebate is zero until someone proves otherwise.

## Why Oregon still works without a $10,000 rebate

Heating degree days in western Oregon are a fraction of Maine or Vermont. Electricity is cheaper than New England. A heat pump that also replaces a tired air conditioner is doing two jobs.

That is why we do not treat Oregon as a rebate story. The [calculator](/calculator/) with an Oregon ZIP is the page that matters. The [heat pump vs furnace](/heat-pump-vs-furnace/) comparison is the one to read if you are on gas.

Eastern Oregon and the Gorge are a different climate than Portland. If your design temperature is closer to a mountain town than to the Willamette Valley, say so on the bid and size for it. Mild-climate equipment in a cold pocket is how Oregon projects disappoint.

## How to use the rebate

Confirm the utility. Confirm the trade ally. Get the incentive code on the bid. Then ignore the rebate for a minute and look at the annual operating comparison. In Oregon the rebate is a nudge. The rate and the climate do the work.
