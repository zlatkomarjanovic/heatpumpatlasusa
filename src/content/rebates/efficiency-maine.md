Maine is the best natural experiment in American home heating. It has the highest share of oil-heated homes in the country, among the highest electricity prices, and the highest per-capita heat pump adoption. Those three facts look contradictory until you do the arithmetic.

## The tiers

Efficiency Maine structures rebates by income, with a per-unit structure and a lifetime cap.

The **standard tier** pays roughly $500 to $1,000 per eligible outdoor unit. The **moderate-income tier** pays $1,000 to $2,000 per unit. The **low-income tier** pays up to $3,000 per unit, within a lifetime limit of $9,000 per housing unit.

That last structure is the one to understand. Because the cap is per housing unit rather than per unit installed, a whole-home conversion with multiple outdoor units can reach the full $9,000. A single-head installation will not. If you are planning a partial installation now and a full conversion later, the lifetime cap means the sequence matters.

## What is paused

Maine opened its federal HEAR allocation and hit the reservation ceiling within weeks. New applications are waitlisted. This is not the same as the program being cancelled, and it is not the same as the money being gone forever, but for planning purposes in 2026 you should assume HEAR money in Maine is not available.

That matters because a lot of content online still advertises Maine's combined incentive totals as if every program were open. If a page is telling you that you can stack $9,000 in Efficiency Maine rebates with $8,000 in HEAR money, that page is describing a funding environment that closed.

## The oil arithmetic that makes Maine work

Maine's residential electricity price is about 29.59 cents per kilowatt hour, which sounds disqualifying. But heating oil at roughly $3.62 a gallon burned at 82 percent efficiency costs about $40 per million BTU of delivered heat, while a cold-climate heat pump on Maine electricity costs around $22 for the same delivered heat.

That is a saving of roughly 45 percent on the largest energy line in the household budget. In a state with 7,826 heating degree days, that is a substantial annual number, and it is why the [oil comparison](/heat-pump-vs-oil/) is the strongest case in the country.

## Sequencing a Maine project

Check the installer's Efficiency Maine participant status first, because it determines eligibility. Confirm the rebate reservation before signing, since the program documentation requirements are enforced. Then decide whether you are doing a whole-home conversion or a staged one, because the lifetime cap rewards doing it in the right order.
