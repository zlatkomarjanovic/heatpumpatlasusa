Connecticut homeowners search **Energize CT heat pump rebate** because that is the brand on the bill inserts. The money is real. It is also smaller than Massachusetts and more dependent on which utility delivers it.

## What Energize CT pays

We model **$500 to $3,500** for a qualifying project. Income and system type move you inside that range. A standard single-zone install for a household that does not qualify for enhanced tiers sits near the bottom. A whole-home or income-qualified project sits higher.

Get the reserved amount on the bid. Energize CT program-year budgets can tighten. A brochure number from last winter is not a commitment.

## Energize CT and Eversource

If your utility is Eversource, read the [Eversource heat pump rebate](/rebates/eversource/) page as well. The statewide rebate and the utility rate are different tools.

The seasonal heat pump rate, where it applies, is the part most quotes underplay. Over ten years a lower heating-season kilowatt-hour price can beat a $2,000 rebate. Ask for both: the rebate reservation and whether your account can move to the heat pump rate after install.

## What not to count

The federal **25C tax credit** is gone for 2026 installs. If a Connecticut quote still includes $2,000 in 25C, subtract it.

## Operating cost in Connecticut

Connecticut electricity is expensive. Oil and propane are also expensive. That pairing still favors the heat pump against oil in most of the state. Against gas, the [calculator](/calculator/) is the honest next step.

Confirm the contractor is participating, confirm the rebate tier, then model the ZIP. The rebate helps. It does not rewrite the rate.
