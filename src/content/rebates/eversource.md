Eversource is a useful case study in how state-level program design dominates utility-level branding. The same company name sits on top of three programs with very different rules and very different amounts.

## Massachusetts

Eversource is a Mass Save sponsor, so a Massachusetts customer does not apply to an Eversource heat pump rebate. They apply through [Mass Save](/rebates/mass-save/), which reaches the largest amounts in the country for whole-home projects.

The thing Eversource actually provides on top is the **seasonal heat pump rate**, a discounted winter electric tariff for qualifying customers. This deserves more attention than it gets. Massachusetts electricity runs 29.61 cents per kilowatt hour, which is among the highest in the continental US. Over a 6,300 degree day winter, a meaningful rate reduction can beat a one-time rebate on total value, and it recurs every year.

## Connecticut

The equivalent program is Energize CT, where heat pump rebates are generally tiered by income and system type and top out lower than Massachusetts. Connecticut electricity is somewhat cheaper than Massachusetts at 24.32 cents per kilowatt hour, but the operating cost case still depends heavily on what you are replacing.

Roughly 40 percent of Connecticut homes heat with oil, which is the highest oil share of any state outside Maine and Vermont. That makes the [oil comparison](/heat-pump-vs-oil/) the relevant one for a large share of Connecticut households, and the case there is strong.

## New Hampshire

New Hampshire programs are administered jointly between the state and utilities, and the federal HEAR allocation has been working through launch during 2026. New Hampshire has expensive electricity at 27.01 cents per kilowatt hour and a 40 percent oil share, which is the same profile that makes Maine's conversion case work.

## How to sequence this

There are two separate processes and they are frequently confused. The **rebate** usually requires pre-approval through the state program before installation. The **rate** requires enrollment after installation with qualifying equipment documented.

Get both in writing before you sign. If a contractor only mentions one, ask about the other. The [calculator](/calculator/) will show you what the operating cost looks like at your current rate, which is the baseline you should compare the heat pump rate against.
