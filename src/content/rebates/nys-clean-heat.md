People search **NYSERDA heat pump rebate**. The program they actually get is **NYS Clean Heat**, run with NYSERDA and the utilities. Same money. Different label. The application goes through a participating contractor in your utility territory, not through a single statewide form.

## What NYS Clean Heat pays

The statewide range we model is about **$1,000 to $8,000** for a qualifying air source heat pump. That spread is real. Con Edison, National Grid, NYSEG, RG&E and the other participating utilities each run their own incentive schedule on top of the statewide rules.

A whole-home, cold-climate system from a participating contractor sits at the high end. A single-zone mini split on a partial-home design sits at the low end. If a quote treats "$8,000" as automatic, ask which tariff and which equipment tier produced that number.

**Con Edison** customers can also see a separate utility rebate, generally **$1,000 to $6,000**, on top of NYS Clean Heat when the project qualifies for both.

## New York HEAR

New York is one of the states with a live HEAR portal. Income-qualified households can receive up to **$8,000** for a heat pump. That is federal money administered in the state, not the Clean Heat rebate under another name.

Treat HEAR as a second reservation, not as a line the contractor will automatically add. If the household does not pass the income screen, the Clean Heat rebate still stands on its own.

## What no longer stacks

The federal **25C tax credit** ended for equipment installed after 2025. Official New York pages still mention Inflation Reduction Act tax credits. For a 2026 install, subtract 25C from any combined figure you were shown last year.

## How to actually get paid

1. Confirm the installer is a participating NYS Clean Heat contractor for your utility.
2. Get the Clean Heat incentive amount in writing, tied to the equipment on the bid.
3. If you might qualify for HEAR, start that application before installation.
4. Run the [calculator](/calculator/) with a New York ZIP so the after-rebate range uses only programs we still mark open.

New York electricity is expensive enough that the rebate is not the whole case. Against oil it usually is still a strong switch. Against gas, model it. The [heat pump vs oil](/heat-pump-vs-oil/) page is the comparison that matters for a large share of upstate housing.
