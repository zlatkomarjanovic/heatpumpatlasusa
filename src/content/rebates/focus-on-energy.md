The **Focus on Energy heat pump rebate** is the Wisconsin utility program people actually search. It is not huge. In a cheap-gas, cold-winter state that honesty matters more than a bigger headline.

## What Focus on Energy pays

We model **$400 to $2,000** for a qualifying residential project. Measure codes and income adders move the number. The catalog is a program-year document. Last year’s PDF is not this year’s reservation.

You generally need a participating Wisconsin utility and a Focus on Energy Trade Ally contractor. Ask for both before you treat the rebate as part of the bid.

## The Midwest gas problem

Wisconsin is not Maine. Residential gas is relatively cheap. Heating degree days are high. A 95 percent furnace on cheap gas can beat a heat pump on running cost in a lot of ZIPs.

That is why this page exists as a rebate page and why the [heat pump vs furnace](/heat-pump-vs-furnace/) model still shows Wisconsin in the contested group. The rebate can close a gap. It does not invent a gap.

The heat pump case gets stronger when:

- You are replacing propane, oil or electric resistance.
- The air conditioner is dying at the same time.
- You want cooling you do not have today.
- A Trade Ally bid uses cold-climate equipment sized for a Wisconsin design temperature, not a catalog two-ton default.

## How to use this page

Confirm the Trade Ally. Get the measure code on the bid. Then run the [calculator](/calculator/) with a Wisconsin ZIP and your actual gas or propane spend. If the model says the furnace still wins, believe it. Publishing that result is the point of this site.

Read the [cold weather guide](/guides/do-heat-pumps-work-in-cold-weather/) before you accept a system that was not sized for a minus-10 night.
