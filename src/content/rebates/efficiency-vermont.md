Vermont is a small state with a serious heating load, a lot of oil and propane, and a rebate program people actually know by name. The **Efficiency Vermont heat pump rebate** is the one that is still open. The federal HEAR number you still see on older blogs is not.

## What Efficiency Vermont pays

We model **$500 to $4,500** for a qualifying project. The spread is mostly income tier and system type, not contractor luck.

A standard ductless or ducted install for a household that does not qualify for enhanced tiers sits toward the bottom. Income-qualified whole-home projects sit toward the top. If a bid writes "$4,500" with no income conversation attached, that number is a hope, not a reservation.

Confirm three things before you sign:

- The installer is a participating Efficiency Vermont contractor.
- The equipment is on the qualifying list.
- The rebate amount on the bid matches a reserved or pre-approved tier, not a brochure maximum.

## Vermont HEAR is waitlisted

Vermont opened HEAR, hit the reservation ceiling, and stopped taking new applications. We still list the $8,000 figure so you can see what other sites are describing. We do **not** add it to the [calculator](/calculator/) total.

If a contractor is still stacking Efficiency Vermont plus HEAR as if both were available, ask them to show a current HEAR reservation. If they cannot, subtract it.

## Why the operating cost case is still strong

Vermont winters are long. Oil and propane remain common outside the gas towns. That is the same arithmetic as Maine, just on a smaller housing stock.

Electricity in Vermont is not cheap. The heat pump still usually wins against oil because oil is worse. Against gas, model it. The [heat pump vs oil](/heat-pump-vs-oil/) page is the comparison that matches most of the rural stock, and the [cold weather guide](/guides/do-heat-pumps-work-in-cold-weather/) is the one to read before you accept a system that was sized like a South Carolina air conditioner.

## Sequencing a Vermont project

Get the Efficiency Vermont reservation first. Decide whether the existing boiler or furnace stays as backup, because that decision can change the tier. Then run the calculator with your actual fuel spend. The rebate is real. It is not large enough to rescue a badly sized system in a 7,000-plus heating degree day climate.
