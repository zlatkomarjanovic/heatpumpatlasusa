What size heat pump do I need? The honest answer is a load calculation, not a square-footage rule of thumb. Sizing is measured in tons (one ton equals 12,000 BTU per hour), and getting it right is the single biggest factor in whether the system feels effortless or struggles. The most common mistake is not undersizing. It is oversizing.

## Why the rule of thumb fails

You will find charts online that say something like one ton per 500 square feet. Those exist because they are easy, not because they are right. Two homes of identical size can have loads that differ by a factor of two, driven by:

- Insulation and air sealing
- Window area, orientation and quality
- Ceiling height and floor plan
- Your design temperature, which depends on your climate

A leaky 1920s house and a tight new build of the same footprint are not the same heating problem. A rule of thumb ignores all of that. Use it only to catch a quote that is obviously wrong, never to specify equipment.

## The real method: Manual J

The industry-standard load calculation is called Manual J, published by the Air Conditioning Contractors of America. It works through the house room by room, accounting for the envelope, the windows, air leakage and the local design temperature, and produces the actual heating and cooling load in BTUs per hour.

A good contractor performs a Manual J and shows you the result. If a bid arrives with a tonnage but no load calculation behind it, that is a red flag. You are being sized by habit, and habit tends to run large.

## Why oversizing is the bigger danger

With furnaces, contractors historically padded the size to be safe. That habit carries over to heat pumps, where it does real harm:

- **Short cycling.** An oversized unit satisfies the thermostat quickly, shuts off, then restarts a few minutes later. That cycling is hard on equipment and uneven for comfort.
- **Poor humidity control.** In cooling mode, a unit that runs in short bursts does not run long enough to pull moisture out of the air, so the house feels clammy even at the set temperature.
- **Lower efficiency.** A single-speed compressor that rarely runs at steady state gives up efficiency it was rated to deliver.

Variable-speed cold-climate units are more forgiving, because they can modulate down. That flexibility is why they are worth the premium, but it is not a licence to deliberately oversize.

## A rough sanity-check table

Use this only to spot a quote that is wildly off, not to specify your system. Actual loads vary widely with envelope and climate.

| Home size | Very rough capacity range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 sq ft | 1 to 1.5 tons | Tighter homes at the low end |
| 1,500 sq ft | 1.5 to 2.5 tons | Climate and insulation move this a lot |
| 2,000 sq ft | 2 to 3 tons | A Manual J often lands lower than people expect |
| 2,500 sq ft | 2.5 to 4 tons | Deep-cold climates push toward the top |

If a contractor's number sits far outside the range for your size and climate, ask to see the load calculation.

## Sizing ductless systems

A [ductless mini split](/guides/ducted-vs-ductless-heat-pump/) is sized per zone. Each indoor head is matched to the load of the room or open area it serves, and then the outdoor unit is confirmed to cover the combined load. The classic ductless sizing mistake is putting an oversized head in a small bedroom, which short cycles and dehumidifies poorly. The same load discipline applies, just at room scale.

## What to ask your contractor

- Did you run a Manual J, and can I see it?
- What design temperature did you use, and why?
- What is the unit's capacity at my cold design temperature, not just at 47 degrees?
- How is backup heat sized, and at what temperature does it engage?

A contractor who can answer those clearly understands the system they are selling. One who cannot is guessing, and guesses run large.

## Bottom line

Size from the load, not the floor area, and be more worried about a unit that is too big than one that is slightly small. Then confirm the cold-weather capacity at your design temperature and a sensible backup plan.
